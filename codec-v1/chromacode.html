The square is a product code

The spec builds a square for a purely dimensional reason: n = ceil(sqrt(L)) is the smallest one that holds the stalk. That shape is also the geometry of a product code. Sum every row, every column and every anti-diagonal, and a single corrupted cell is not merely detected — it is located, at the intersection of the sums that disagree.

Anti-diagonals are not decoration here. Cell weight in this representation depends only on r + c, so the anti-diagonals are the place values — and they are what catch the four-corner rectangle that defeats plain row and column parity.

What the sums say

—

+1 0 −1 parity sum

The rim carries N row sums, N column sums and 2N-1 anti-diagonal sums — 4N-1 for N^2 cells, so the overhead is (4N-1)/N^2: 64% at N=6 but 12.4% at N=32. Turn the anti-diagonals off and press Four corners: every row and column sum stays balanced, and the damage becomes invisible. That is what the extra vector buys.
