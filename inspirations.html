Who got here first

Inspirations

Maths is discovery, not invention. Nearly every piece of this site was found before — often decades before, sometimes by engineers solving an unrelated problem. This page names them. Arriving somewhere already furnished does not make the walk less real.

The digits

Red −1, green 0, blue +1 in base two — a redundant representation, where the same number has many spellings.

digits −1, 0, +1 in base 2 Signed-digit representation · Balanced ternary is its base-3 cousin
Booth, 1951
adding two grids in one pass, no carries Redundant binary representation — the redundancy is what buys carry-free addition
Avizienis, 1961. The 20,000-pair test was rediscovering his theorem.
push — spreading colour as far as it goes Booth’s multiplication algorithm recodes runs the same way
NAF — the fewest lit cells Non-adjacent form · used to make elliptic-curve multiplication a third faster

The stalks

Blue and red edges read as a number, and the +/− address that names it.

blue–red stalks with values Hackenbush · Surreal numbers
Conway, On Numbers and Games, 1976; Berlekamp–Conway–Guy, Winning Ways, 1982
the + − + − address The sign expansion — a sign expansion of a surreal number is exactly a Hackenbush chain. Combinatorial game theory
pinning 2⁰ so everything is a fraction Dyadic rationals — exactly the numbers with finite birthdays
The standard Hackenbush recipe already puts the binary point at the first colour change.

The atlas

Rings of integers, ordered by value — which turned out to be an FFT and a machine part.

bits reversed over 2L Van der Corput sequence, 1935 · the base of every low-discrepancy sequence
why counting scrambles the arc Bit-reversal permutation — the input reordering of the Cooley–Tukey FFT, 1965
ring r is 2r+1 even directions Roots of unity — the FFT’s twiddle factors, in polar form
concentric rings, each doubling its sectors An absolute rotary encoder disc · often in Gray code so only one track changes at a time
Engineers machined this geometry into shafts because it is the efficient way to read an angle.

Shifting

Move the point, or drop a digit — two different operations, both classical.

dropping a digit; orbits and periods The doubling map · Symbolic dynamics
bar notation reaching thirds Repeating expansions · block length divides 2m−1 by the multiplicative order of 2
a binary tree of every rational Stern–Brocot tree

The wub ±

Phasors summed tip to tail, riding surfaces, tying knots.

phasors summed tip to tail tracing a closed curve Epicycles · Fourier series · Phasors
Ptolemy had the picture; Fourier had the theorem.
(p,q) from two rates Torus knots on a torus · Knot theory
counting crossings in a projection Crossing number — the minimum over all diagrams, which is why ours is only an upper bound

The fold

Anti-diagonals, mirror symmetry, and inversion in a circle.

constant along anti-diagonals Hankel matrices
the fold-mirror J Aᵀ J Persymmetric matrices — symmetric about the anti-diagonal
fixes the Fold, swaps Inner and Outer, undoes itself Inversion in a circle — those three properties are its definition
laying a digit string along the anti-diagonals and cutting it into Inner / Fold / Outer, so the three sum back to the number exactly Not placed. Anti-diagonal traversal is a stock algorithm and folding a matrix over a diagonal is patented, but neither does this partition.
Held loosely — a few searches is thin evidence, and this sort of thing hides in course notes and personal sites.
Every link above goes to Wikipedia. The parts are old; the instrument is not. Nobody arrives at Avizienis by staring at grids, or at Conway by colouring sticks, or at the FFT by asking why the rings came out scrambled — but that is the route this took, and the rooms were furnished when we got there.
