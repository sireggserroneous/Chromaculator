Inspirations

Maths is discovery, not invention. Nearly every piece of this site was found before — often decades before, sometimes by engineers solving an unrelated problem. This page names them. Arriving somewhere already furnished does not make the walk less real.

The anatomy of 0

Where the digits come from — and why they cannot start any earlier.

0 is not a point. Between −0 and +0 sits an entire structure of games, and the numbers on the rest of this site only begin once you are out of it. The order, Red to Blue, with every step ascending:

−0 < tiny-on < tiny < up < over < +ε < [ every real ] < ±ω < [ every real ] < −ε < under < down < miny < miny-on < +0

It is not linearly ordered, and that is the point. The game ∗ = {0 | 0} is not less than 0, not greater than 0, and not equal to it — it is confused with it. Trichotomy fails, so there is no side to be on. Left and right are total-order words and cannot be used in here. Red and Blue can, because they name whose advantage rather than which direction — which is also the Winning Ways convention: bLue is Left, Red is Right. The vocabulary did not happen to fit. It is the only vocabulary that can.

The rim is made of the centre. tiny-on is not tiny stacked on tiny. on = {on |} is a game, pronounced omega, and it is above everything; tiny-on is tiny parameterised by it — the smallest thing you can build, made out of the biggest thing there is. So the structure closes on itself. Stretch the pinhole at 0 open and look down, and on is what is at the bottom, because it was the material all along. That is inversion in a circle: the map that swaps a disc’s centre with its boundary. Every positive infinitesimal is greater than 1/on, so the floor of the whole zoo is the reciprocal of what sits at its middle.

Zero is doubled and ω is unified, and that asymmetry is a choice. Floating point doubles both; the Riemann sphere unifies both. Doubling only zero is what makes this a disc with a centre rather than a sphere, and what turns the chain above into a closed loop rather than a line.

The bits cannot start until you are out. A sign expansion is a number’s address, and ∗, ↑ and tiny are not numbers — they have no position for a bit string to point at. Everything else on this site begins exactly where this zoo ends, and that is forced by the theory rather than chosen. Meanwhile everything ordinarily called a number — the counting numbers, the reals, the irrationals, all of analysis — is the single greyed band above, between ε and ω. One step of thirteen. All the structure is at the two ends.

a game confused with 0 — neither side of it Star · Nimbers, its family
Which is why the ordering is a partial order and trichotomy fails.
up, down, and the infinitesimals below every number Up · Infinitesimals
↑ is positive and smaller than every positive number — below 1/ω, below 1/ω², below all of them.
tiny, miny, over, under, on Berlekamp–Conway–Guy, Winning Ways vol. 1 p. 126 for tiny and miny; Conway, On Numbers and Games ch. 11 for the loopy games over, under and on
The book writes tiny-x as +x and miny-x as −x — the two halves are called plus and minus at the source. miny is exactly −tiny, so the halves of the disc are one negation apart.
up sitting just above the tinies It is one: +0 = {0 | {0 | 0}} = {0 | ∗} = ↑, Winning Ways vol. 1 p. 126
A tiny shrinks as its parameter grows, faster than any multiple can climb back. So up, with parameter 0, is the largest tiny, and tiny-on — parameter on, the largest there is — is the smallest. The chain is a single family, not a stack of species.
Red and Blue instead of left and right Combinatorial game theory names the players bLue and Red
The colour convention here is theirs, arrived at for the same reason: a partial order has no directions, only players.
−0 and +0 as two places, outside the disc Signed zero · IEEE 754 has both, and 1/+0 = +∞ while 1/−0 = −∞
The reciprocal sends the two outer rings to the centre. Floating point got there for engineering reasons.
one ω in the middle, the line closed into a loop Real projective line · Riemann sphere · ω and the ordinals
looking through the pinhole to see the centre Inversion in a circle · a conformal map exchanging the origin with the boundary

The atlas

Rings of integers, ordered by value — which turned out to be an FFT, a machine part, and a hyperbolic plane.

bits reversed over 2L Van der Corput sequence, 1935 · the base of every low-discrepancy sequence
why counting scrambles the arc Bit-reversal permutation — the input reordering of the Cooley–Tukey FFT, 1965
ring r is 2r+1 even directions Roots of unity — the FFT’s twiddle factors, in polar form
concentric rings, each doubling its sectors An absolute rotary encoder disc · often in Gray code so only one track changes at a time
Engineers machined this geometry into shafts because it is the efficient way to read an angle.
big in the middle, small at the rim, a boundary never reached The Poincaré disc model of hyperbolic geometry · the rim is an ideal point at infinite distance
Linear radius against a population that doubles each ring is the signature: circumference growing like e^(r ln 2) puts the curvature at −(ln 2)² ≈ −0.48. That is why it is a horizon, not a wall.

Multiplication and division

One is a rectangle you read; the other is the same rectangle, searched for.

every cell of A against every cell of B, no carries The grid method — the box taught in primary school · Outer product · Multiplication algorithms
It works because a product of sums expands. Division has no such expansion, which is the whole difference.
squashing the rectangle: sum the anti-diagonals, then carry Lattice multiplication — gelosia, the method Napier’s bones mechanise
The rectangle is the lattice; the squash is its diagonal sum. Which is also where the carries the grid deferred come due — about two grids in three owe one.
rows of B, shifted and subtracted, one yes-or-no each Long division — the tableau is the multiplication rectangle sheared, so its anti-diagonals become columns you can subtract one at a time
stopping early and keeping the odd chunk out Euclidean division — quotient and remainder, exact at every width, instead of an expansion that never ends
The oldest idea on this page, and still the one doing the most work.
a stalk in (−1,1) with a multiplier on the boundary Floating point · the significand normalised into a band, the exponent naming which band
Arrived at from the other direction, which suggests the layout is forced. The difference: floating point rounds inside the band and forgets. This keeps the remainder.

Every link above goes to Wikipedia. The parts are old; the instrument is not. Nobody arrives at Avizienis by staring at grids, or at Conway by colouring sticks, or at the FFT by asking why the rings came out scrambled — but that is the route this took, and the rooms were furnished when we got there.
